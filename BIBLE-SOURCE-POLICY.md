# Bible source review

Apply one source standard to every book. Keep the shared reading, map, source dialogs, and perspective controls.

## Required review

Read the relevant source passage before writing a note. Record the author, publisher, edition or date, direct URL, chapter coverage, review date, and access limits. Attach each note to the chapters it actually discusses. A source's reputation does not verify every claim associated with it.

Keep the biblical account, material evidence, scholarly interpretation, Church teaching, and original reflection distinct. Do not call an interpretation established history. Do not use a geographic atlas as evidence that an event occurred.

## Wikipedia

Use Wikipedia for encyclopedia background and source discovery. Record the reviewed revision and link to it. Check relevant cited scholarship through its publisher, university, museum, or original publication. Write short original paraphrases. Preserve attribution and the CC BY-SA license link. Review each image's separate license. Do not treat Wikipedia as the final authority for disputed dates, identities, locations, or authorship.

## Dan McClellan

Use relevant original videos, interviews, publications, or posts. Review the relevant transcript or captions and record timestamps. Identify transcription errors and access limits. Do not copy Hebrew or Greek from automatic captions. Verify cited works through their original publication or publisher. State whether the full work, an abstract, a description, or an indirect excerpt was read. Identify McClellan's interpretation as scholarly commentary, separate from Church teaching. Do not claim independent verification of a consensus from one speaker's claim.

## Church sources

Use official Church pages for Come, Follow Me, Church manuals, and Restoration scripture. Record the edition year. Match Come, Follow Me to its selected chapter coverage. Do not apply a lesson to omitted chapters merely because they are in the same book. Identify older Institute commentary as older commentary. Do not present Church interpretation as independent archaeological evidence. Keep later scripture connections distinct from the Bible passage's own words.

## Verified scholarship

Prefer original papers, academic books, university lectures, museum catalogues, and object records. Verify author, title, date, publisher, and relevant passage. State disagreements and uncertainty. A publisher description verifies a work's identity and scope, not every argument in its full text. A modern terrain map does not establish ancient coastlines, campsites, or roads.

## Release review

Every ready chapter must include an original close reading of a selected local Scripture passage. Review the surrounding verses before choosing its anchor. Explain the chapter's form, social situation, or consequences rather than repeating its events. Record coverage separately from the review of external scholarship.

Original close readings use the local World English Bible as their primary text. A complete chapter inventory does not mean every external linked work received a new review. Preserve each existing source's actual access and review limits. New archaeological or historical assertions require reviewed primary evidence.

Use real artifact photographs only where their identity and passage connection are clear. State whether an object directly relates to the account or supplies a regional comparison. Preserve collection identity, object date, dimensions, credits, and reuse rights. Use `imageChapters` to limit image display independently of broader source associations. Do not require an image where no relevant verified object is available.

Review narrative notes, person descriptors, dates, relationships, location claims, route claims, and image credits. Sources must support the specific claim. Do not add irrelevant material to fill a category. Leave inaccessible or unreviewed material out of the published guide. Document remaining review work rather than marking it verified.
